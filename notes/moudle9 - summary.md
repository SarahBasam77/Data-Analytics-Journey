# Types of Bias in Data Analysis
## Bias is a human tendency that can skew data analysis. Five key types are:

- **Confirmation Bias:** Making choices that favor proving a preconceived hypothesis, influencing data selection and conclusions.
  
- **Selection Bias:** Results from using incomplete or non-representative data (e.g., seasonal data used out of context).
  
- **Interpretation Bias:** Occurs from misinterpreting the data or the question, often when analysis is ambiguous.
  
- **Information Bias:** Caused by flaws in the data source itself (e.g., incomplete measurement like ad click data) or by the undue influence of outliers.
  
- **Predictive Bias:** Caused by using historical data collected under conditions that no longer apply, leading to inaccurate predictions.
  
- **Avoiding Bias:** Key strategies include being aware of bias, documenting assumptions, validating data sources, reviewing patterns and outliers separately, seeking peer review, and remaining impartial.

# Data Privacy & Security Regulations
Data regulations are evolving globally, differing by country.
Global Landscape: As of late 2021, 71% of countries have data security laws, while others are drafting or lack legislation.

# Key Regulations:

- **GDPR (EU):** A comprehensive, restrictive regulation governing personal data processing for individuals in the EU. It defines personal data broadly.
  
- **PII (Globally):** Many countries protect Personally Identifiable Information (PII), data that can identify an individual.
  
- **Compliance:** Analysts must be aware of applicable privacy and copyright laws before using data.

### Ethical Scenarios:  complex issues around data ownership (medical records), public benefit vs. individual rights (surveillance), and informed consent (data resale in EULAs).

# Data Security: The CIA Triad
## The CIA triad is a core model for data security:

1. **Confidentiality** (Privacy): Ensuring data is accessible only to authorized individuals through methods like encryption, authentication, and employee training.
   
2. **Integrity:** Maintaining the accuracy and trustworthiness of data throughout its lifecycle. Ensured by access controls, version control, backups, and integrity checks like hashing (using an algorithm to create a unique fingerprint for data).
   
3. **Availability:** Guaranteeing authorized users have reliable access to data and networks. Maintained through system upkeep, disaster recovery plans, and security measures like firewalls.

# Quick-Reference: Bias Examples
- **Information Bias:** A dataset where 3 accounts with over $100,000 distort the average for 1000 accounts with under $5,000.
  
- **Selection Bias:** A brand awareness survey conducted only in gyms and health food stores, excluding the general population.
  
- **Confirmation Bias:** An analyst selecting only data they believe will support their initial hypothesis.
  
- **Interpretation Bias:** Assuming a friend is angry after they walk past without speaking, without considering other explanations.
